# Methodological decisions

Every significant choice made in the project, with the reason or the evidence behind it. Notebook references: D1–D12 in `02`, D13–D14 in `03`, D15–D16 in `04`, D17 in `05`, D18–D20 in `06`, D21 in `09`, D22–D25 in `10`, D26–D29 in `11`, `14`; D30–D33 in `11`, `12`; D34–D37 in `13`; D38 in `17`; D39 in `18`; D40 in `20`; D41–D42 in `21`.

| # | Decision | Rationale / caveat |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Unit of analysis = one consolidated row per player-season (`LeagueDashPlayerStats`); `TEAM_COUNT` kept as a traded flag | Player development is the object of study. Team context for traded players needs per-team splits, which we'll add only if needed. |
| D2 | Request totals and derive rates ourselves; take possession-based rates (USG%, AST%, …) from NBA.com | Totals add up across seasons and stints, which rolling windows and shrinkage need. |
| D3 | Height/weight = **as listed each season** (`LeagueDashPlayerBioStats`) | Time-correct. Caveat: the NBA switched to measured heights in 2019-20 (56% of players got shorter, mean −0.57 in), so there is a level shift at that season. |
| D4 | Listed position = **as listed each season** (`CommonTeamRoster`), never `PlayerIndex` | `PlayerIndex` returns current values for any season, which leaks future information. Roster position is missing for about 9% of player-seasons (about 1.4% of minutes). |
| D5 | Also build a **derived continuous position spectrum** from same-season stats; evaluate against listed position | Listed labels are coarse (G/F/C + hybrids). Where derived and listed position disagree may itself be a "unicorn" signal. |
| D6 | Age = exact age on **Feb 1** of the season (from roster birth date); NBA integer age as fallback (`age_source`) | Mid-season convention. Birth date is a fixed fact, so it can come from any season's roster without leakage. |
| D7 | **No rows dropped** for low minutes; reliability columns (`min`, `poss`, `fga`, `fta`, `fg3a`) kept | Minimum-minute thresholds are a modelling choice. They will be toggleable and their effect examined. |
| D8 | Playoffs in a **separate table** (`player_season_playoffs`), same key | Not every player has playoff data, and keeping it apart prevents accidental leakage. Planned use: does *early* playoff experience predict development? |
| D9 | Draft info from `DraftHistory` (by player ID), not the bio endpoint | The bio endpoint carries wrong draft records for some players (e.g. Walker Russell Jr. has his father's 1982 pick). |
| D10 | **When undecided, keep the feature**; redundancy is flagged, not deleted | Overfitting can be pruned later; information dropped too early cannot be recovered. Final selection happens inside each walk-forward window. |
| D11 | Shooting percentages shrunk with a **beta-binomial prior per season × listed-position group** (`*_shr`) | Validated on the first training window: 29–53% lower next-season error than raw. |
| D12 | **Season-relative** z-score and percentile for every feature, scaled on that season's ≥500-min players (`*_z`, `*_pctl`); raw kept | League 3PA rate rose from 0.22 to 0.42 and mid-range rate fell from 0.29 to 0.09 over 2011-26. |
| D13 | Season-to-season change is measured against the **previous season played**, with `seasons_missed` recording gaps | Keeps injury comebacks (e.g. Klay Thompson 2018-19 → 2021-22) instead of blanking them; the gap itself is informative. |
| D14 | Trajectories: 3-season rolling **level** and **trend** (shooting % weighted by attempts, other stats by minutes) plus **vs-own-past** deviation; outlier seasons kept as they happened | Level, direction and "unusual for him" are different signals. No player-history shrinkage: the actual season is kept and flagged. |
| D15 | Position spectrum on one 1 (G) → 5 (C) scale: **listed**, **body** (height/weight) and **style** (stats only); ridge models fitted on seasons ≤ *t* | Gaps between body and style are a direct unicorn signal (e.g. LeBron, Jokić). Out-of-sample R² vs listed position: style 0.68–0.79, body 0.76–0.83, both declining as the league becomes positionless. |
| D16 | Two unicorn scores, each relative to that season only: **combination** (summed excess rarity of trait pairs a player is top-20% in) and **strangeness** (mean distance to his 5 most similar rotation players) | Rejected after testing: "rarest single pair" (saturates; rewards being #1 at one skill) and robust Mahalanobis distance (treats the whole cluster of traditional centres as outliers). Headline lists require ≥ median impact; raw scores kept. |
| D17 | **Own star metric**: six equally weighted pillars (scoring, creation, rebounding, defense, impact, trust) from that season only, half-adjusted for body size (dial 0.5); star season = top 5% (≥ 1,500 scaled min). Breakout labels: production, role, scoring and any, predicted one season ahead; star predicted within 3 seasons | PIE over-represents bigs (27% of PIE stars vs 14% of players) and media awards carry their own biases. At dial 0.5 bigs are 18% and guards 33% of star seasons. |
| D18 | **Walk-forward backtest**: test season *t* uses features ≤ *t*; a training row from season *s* is used only if its outcome was known by *t* (*s* + horizon ≤ *t*); preprocessing fitted per fold. Population: all players aged ≤ 25 (stars excluded for the star outcome). Metrics: average precision and lift, top-*k* hit rate | Breakouts are rare (1–7%), so accuracy is meaningless. First results: any breakout 3.4×, scoring 4.8×, star-within-3 10× (current star score). |
| D19 | Production models: **boosted trees** for breakout probabilities (best lift and calibration); **current star score** for star potential; **nearest comps** for explanation only | Backtest with player-bootstrap CIs: trees 4.2× any / 6.1× scoring; star score 10× for star-within-3, beating every trained model; inner-window tuning did not help. |
| D20 | **No minimum-minutes filter** on the prediction population | Cut-offs leave the top-10 hit rate unchanged (36–40%) but drop up to 17% of future breakouts and 19% of future stars. Breakout-season minutes (900/1,200/1,500) don't change conclusions. |
| D21 | **Two routes to the star tier** (top 10% by star score, within 3 seasons): *breakout route* (≥ 1 breakout season on the way) vs *steady route* (no breakout season, ≥ 10 percentile-point climb from the latest known level). Repeated breakouts are kept as-is | Captures stars like Anthony Edwards who never 'break out' but climb consistently. Hard breakout thresholds kept for now (e.g. Anthony Black at 14.98 ppg just misses the 15-ppg scoring bar). |
| D22 | **MIP baseline** = the player's most recent previous season covering ≥ 33% of that season's games | Injury-shortened seasons made comebacks look like leaps (Kawhi Leonard 2018-19 vs a 9-game 2017-18; Derrick Rose 2018-19 vs 25 games). |
| D23 | **MIP eligibility**: ≥ 1 previous season, a baseline season, and at least half the season's games; the 65-game award rule is ignored | Second-year players reach the real ballot (19% of the voting top 7), so excluding them capped any comparison with the voting. |
| D24 | **MIP model**: conditional logit (one winner per season) on ΔX and Δ% of every stat, team win % change, age and a second-year flag; L2 penalty chosen by leave-one-season-out | ΔX beats Δ%; team, age and the second-year flag each improve out-of-sample fit. Level-reached inputs and milestones did not help pick the winner. |
| D25 | **Voting results** (Basketball-Reference) used only to evaluate and display our top 7, never to train | Keeps the model's inputs to on-court data; the vote is the thing being explained. |
| D26 | **Preseason form** = per-36 rate minus last season's rate, multiplied by minutes ÷ (minutes + 150), centred within each preseason. Preseason minute increases (decreases ignored) were tested and left out | Preseason minutes are not real: increases hold at 60–75%, decreases at about 20%. Minute increases did not improve the winner's rank once per-36 form was known. |
| D27 | ~~Preseason MIP candidates exclude previous winners and players who averaged 22+ points last season~~ **Revised 30 Oct 2026:** no exclusions. Established scorers are handled by a **scoring turning point**: last season's points count for a player up to 20 a game and against him above it. Penalty λ = 3 | No winner averaged more than 20.3 the season before. Every turning point from 14 to 26 was backtested; 20 fit best in both the leave-one-out and the strict test (5.6× and 3.3× a blind guess, against 5.1× and 2.9× with none). Same turning point used in the live race (notebook 12), where it improved fit but put the winner in the mid-January top 7 in 4 of 5 seasons rather than 5. Previous winners stay in: nothing bars a second award. λ = 3: a weaker penalty scored better on 14 winners but that is tuning on the answers. |
| D28 | **Opportunity** (share of the team's minutes freed up over the summer, from preseason rosters only) tested as a preseason input and **left out** | A tie: same overall fit in both tests, one more winner in the top 20, slightly worse median. Shown as a descriptive fact in notebook 12. |
| D29 | **Preseason input: a 20-point scorer left his team and none arrived** (yes/no; known from preseason rosters: the star is seen with another team, and no 20+ scorer has joined) | Notebook 14: 7 of 21 winners had a 20-point scorer leave (vs 11% of candidates); changing team alone made no difference. In the preseason model it moved the winner into the top 7 in 7 of 14 seasons (from 3) and the strict fit from 3.3× to 4.9×. Four scoring bands (20-22/22-25/25-27/27+) fitted better on paper but learned a shape with no basketball sense (losing a 21- or 28-point scorer 'hurts'), from 6 cases; graded versions were no better than yes/no. |
| D30 | **Penalty λ = 3 confirmed** for the MIP choice models (notebooks 11, 12, 15) | Chosen on fit, not on how many winners it ranks first; a weaker penalty looked better on 14 winners but that is tuning on the answers. |
| D31 | **Archetypes are found from the data first, with no expected list written beforehand** | A prewritten list would steer what we look for and push players into bands we already believe in. Clusters are named only after they are found, from their style measures and example players. |
| D32 | **Live race (notebook 12): the data chooses the scoring turning point** from none, 16, 18, 20, 22 or 24 points a game. It is re-chosen every day by season-grouped cross-validation on past seasons only, and **frozen at the All-Star break**. The backtests (12A, 12D) choose it inside each training set | Replaces a fixed 20 that was chosen on preseason data. Same leak-proof method as notebook 15, tested on 21 seasons rather than 5. Freezing at the break stops the leaderboard jumping late in the race. To 29 October it has chosen 20 on every day, so nothing has changed yet. |
| D33 | **The blip test** (first used for Nickeil Alexander-Walker), **written down on 30 October 2026 before the answer is known.** Share of the 9.4 → 20.8 leap kept at the All-Star break: 80%+ real, 50–80% partly real, under 50% blip; at least 25 games, otherwise decide at season end with the same cut-offs. Points per 36 shown, not used | The cut-offs come from the 14 past winners (9 kept 80%+; 3 kept under 40%). For all 14, the verdict at the break of the season after matched the full-season verdict, so the break is early enough. **Backtest on 609 leaps since 2000-01 (notebook 16):** 86% match; about 1 blip in 5 is real again two seasons on (e.g. Randle), and about 1 real in 6 collapses later. Rule left unchanged after seeing this. |
| D34 | **Archetype set-up (notebook 13):** 15 style measures, body measures left out (shown afterwards); each measure compared within its own season; player-seasons with 500+ minutes, 2011-12 → 2025-26; soft membership (Gaussian mixture, k-means as a check). **The number of clusters is not fixed in advance:** plotted from 2 to 14 and chosen by looking. **No names up front:** clusters are numbered, and each is named later from its three most typical players | No expected list (D31) and no fixed count, so the data isn't forced into types we already believe in. 13B: style is mostly a continuum; 3 clusters (stability 0.94) and 5 (0.87) come back from random halves of the data, while 7+ don't (0.5–0.6). |
| D35 | **Eight playing-style clusters** (notebook 13D); five kept as the coarser view (13C). Named later from their best-known and most typical players | Less stable on 2011-26 alone (0.54 against 0.87 for five), but the eight fit neatly inside the five and each split has a basketball reading (stretch bigs; corner 3-and-D; two on-ball groups). The 2000-26, 3-season-average test will show whether they hold. |
| D36 | **Archetypes over 2000-01 → 2025-26 on 3-season rolling windows, no era adjustment** (notebook 13E). Totals pooled over the window's three seasons, then turned into the 15 style rates; included when the player had 500+ minutes in the window's last season; measures standardised once over all windows (scale only); 8 clusters | Shows the league's drift directly: the traditional rim big fell from 23% of players to 5%, the corner 3-and-D wing rose from 4% to 20%. Less stable than 2011-26 within-season clusters (0.45 vs 0.54 at 8, halves split by player). NBA.com has all 15 measures back to 1998-99. |
| D37 | **Names for the eight 2011-26 clusters (13D):** 1 Mr. Inside the Key · 2 Windex · 3 Bruiser · 4 The Pest · 5 Give Me the Ball and Let Me Work · 6 Peter Griffin's NBA Jam · 7 Kerr's Cult · 8 The Effective Operator | Named after seeing each cluster's best-known and most typical players (D31: no names up front). The 2000-26 clusters (13E) stay numbered; they overlap with these but not one to one. |
| D38 | **"Already an All-Star" counts against a Most Improved candidate:** `prior_allstar` = 1 if picked for any All-Star game before that season (never the current one), added to the preseason model (notebook 11) and the live race (notebook 12). Selections: Basketball-Reference All-Star pages for names to check, then NBA.com PlayerAwards by player_id | Notebook 17: 0 of 26 winners since 2000-01 were prior All-Stars (about 1 candidate in 6 is), against about 2 wins the model expected. It improved every test: preseason strict median winner rank 15 → 7 (top 7 in 5 of 9, was 4); live race day 85 top 7 in 12 of 15 (was 11); 2000-26 mid-season top 7 in 17 of 21 (was 16). A penalty, not an exclusion (odds ×0.25), in line with D27. Count of selections and a wider 'established' measure (All-NBA, awards) not used: no extra evidence, more room to overfit 26 awards. The live race's daily file was rebuilt from day 1 (no leak: selections before the season are known on opening night). |
| D39 | **"Back from a lost season" counts against a Most Improved candidate:** `stale` = 1 if the baseline season is older than last season (last season was lost or injury-shortened, so D22 skipped it). Added to the live race (notebook 12) and the preseason model (notebook 11). Tested alongside and **rejected**: older hot starts counting for less (age × early jump), how much of the jump already showed in the short season ('head start'), and share of games played | Notebook 18: no player aged 27+ coming off a bench role and up 8+ points at day 19 has won (14 lookalikes; 31 aged 27+ any role, one winner). Notebook 18C: of the candidate inputs only `stale` improved fit in all 10 tests (days 14-85, strict and leave-one-out); 0 of 26 winners had a stale baseline (about 1.2 expected; chance of 0 about 30%, so the case rests on consistency, not on the count). Preseason: strict fit 5.6× → 6.0×, leave-one-out top 7 in 9 of 14 (was 8). Effect today: Ty Jerome 26.5% → 8.3%, still #1. Odds ×0.2 in-season. A penalty, not an exclusion (D27). Daily file rebuilt from day 1 (no leak: whether last season was lost is known on opening night). |
| D40 | **No recalibration of the Most Improved chances; a note for big early favourites instead.** The live race prints a note when a chance is above 25% before day 45: such favourites have won about 1 time in 6 (re-checked 12 November with the live race's current inputs, D41 and D42: 19 such favourites, about 7.5 expected wins, 3 won; the decision stands) | Notebook 20, out-of-sample 2000-01 → 2025-26 at ten points of the season: chances are honest across the bands (1–3% won about 2%, 3–10% about 6–8%, 10–25% about 15–19%); the temperature the data asks for is 0.86–1.02, and applying it (learned without the season it is tested on) lowers the fit at every point; the #1's gap between expected and actual wins is within luck (32–64%). The one real exception: 19 early favourites (25%+, days 7–30) had about 7.6 expected wins and 3 actual (luck alone: about 2%). Shrinking only those chances would be fitted to 19 cases, so the honest response is a note, not a correction. |
| D41 | **The live race's turning point moves from points to game score; the preseason model keeps points.** The in-season model (notebook 12) now uses the baseline season's game score, both as a straight input and with a turning point chosen daily from 12.5–18.5 or none (the same percentiles of candidates as 16–24 points; D32 otherwise unchanged). The preseason model (notebook 11) keeps last season's points above 20 (D27) | Notebook 21B, 12 November 2026. Each measure's turning point was chosen inside each training set. By the rule written before the results (replace points only if better in the strict test at most points of the season, with no fewer winners in the top 7), game score won in-season at 2 of 3 points (day 19 6.3× → 6.4×; season's end 23.4× → 24.4×; day 85 19.4× → 19.2×, one fewer in the top 7). Before the season nothing beat points; usage was worse. The gains are small: baseline points and game score correlate 0.955. The finding is that voters hold established all-round production against a player, not scoring as such |
| D42 | **"Already clearly a star" counts against a Most Improved candidate in the live race:** two inputs from the baseline season, both known on opening night: `elite_level` (baseline game score 16 or more) and `base_star_tier` (baseline season in our star tier, the top 10% by star score; notebook 05). Added alongside "already an All-Star" (D38). Star scores start in 2011-12, so earlier baseline seasons count as 0 | Notebook 21C–21D, 12 November 2026. The flag's threshold was chosen inside each training set from 16, 18, 20 or 22, and was 16 in nearly every window. All three options passed the rule written in advance (better in the strict test at 2 of 3 points of the season, no fewer winners in the top 7). The first tie-break (larger average gain) compared 9 seasons with 21, so it was replaced, before the deciding test, by a comparison on the same seasons (2012-13 → 2025-26): both together +6.4% strict and +7.8% leave-one-out, against +4.3% / +3.9% for the flag and +2.4% / +4.5% for the star tier. Caveats: the evidence is thinner than for D38 (the previous model expected only about 0.2 wins from players with a baseline game score of 18+, against 0 actual), and at day 85 the winner was in the top 3 slightly less often (5 seasons against 6). Raised after Victor Wembanyama and Cooper Flagg ranked high in the live race; decided by the test, not by the board |
